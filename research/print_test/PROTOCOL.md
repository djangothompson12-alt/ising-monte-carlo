# Printed-tile camera test: protocol (DRAFT, not yet committed as final)

**Status: draft written with AI assistance on 21 November 2026. Django must
read, edit and commit the final version before any tile is photographed. The
commit time is the pre-registration record.**

## Question

Does the pixel threshold found with simulated block-averaging cameras hold
when the images come from a real camera, with its own optics, sensor
processing and blind (Otsu) thresholding?

## Fixed before photographing

- Tiles: the 12 tiles generated by `make_tiles.py` at the commit that adds this
  file. Truth: `truth.json` from that run.
- Measurement: `analyse_photos.py` at the same commit. No change to the
  segmentation, crop or length estimator after the first photo.
- Settings: at least five distances per composition, spanning about 4 to 0.4
  pixels per site. All six tiles of a composition at one setting.
- Every photo taken is listed and analysed. A photo may be retaken only for a
  reason recorded before analysis (out of focus, wrong tile, frame cut off,
  aspect warning).

## Predictions

1. At any setting where every tile has at least 3 pixels per domain, the
   photo exponent is within 0.01 of the printed truth.
2. At any setting where the median is below 1 pixel per domain, the photo
   exponent is lower than the truth.
3. Settings between 1 and 3 pixels per domain are reported without a
   pass/fail rule.

Secondary, descriptive only: compare each photo error with the block-average
model's error at the same pixels per domain, to see whether the real camera is
better or worse than the mathematical one.

## Limits

One trajectory per composition, so this tests the measurement, not the
statistics of the simulation. Phone processing (sharpening, noise reduction)
is part of the tested chain and is not controlled. Plastic tiles under a phone
camera are not a metallographic image.
