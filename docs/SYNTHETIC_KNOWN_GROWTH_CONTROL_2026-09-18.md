# A known-growth control for the image measurement

*AI-assisted exploratory analysis, 18 September 2026. This is a check on a
measurement method, not a new Kawasaki simulation, alloy experiment, or
student-authored paper paragraph. The design was tried locally before this
note was written; it is not preregistered.*

## Why this control matters

The Kawasaki snapshots do not come with a known, exact coarsening exponent at
each finite time. To check what the **image** length estimator itself can do,
the script `research/synthetic_growth_control.py` creates alternating square
domains with side length `2s` and labels their synthetic time `t=s³`. Their
geometric width therefore follows **exactly** `t^(1/3)` by construction.
This is a deliberately simple mathematical pattern, not a model of a real
microstructure. The field widths are 384, 768 and 1536 sites; each contains
whole repeats of the pattern at every tested scale. Six scales, 6–32 sites,
are retained. The same non-periodic, finite-window correlation half-height
method used by the applied-image audit measures every image. No fit window,
pattern or unresolved result is discarded after measuring it.

## What it returned

| Field width (sites) | Geometric exponent | Native measured exponent | 4× block + threshold | 8× block + threshold |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 384 | 0.333333 | 0.354671 | 0.354908 | 0.352756 |
| 768 | 0.333333 | 0.343603 | 0.343745 | 0.340217 |
| 1536 | 0.333333 | 0.338373 | 0.338458 | 0.334221 |

All six points were resolved in every condition. There are no error bars:
these are deterministic images, not independent experimental specimens or
stochastic trajectories. The measured exponent approaches the imposed value
as the field gets wider, while downsampling has relatively little effect on
**this particular aligned checkerboard**. That small downsampling effect is
worth keeping: it shows the large binning shifts on the Kawasaki snapshots
are not a universal property of every two-colour image. The native shift at
finite field width also shows that an apparent exponent difference can arise
even though the generating growth law is unchanged.

Do **not** infer from this that the Kawasaki result below 1/3 is an imaging
artefact. Its periodic engine length is a different estimator; its patterns
are rough and evolving; and this control's finite-image effect shifts the
known exponent **upward**, not toward the observed low slopes. The value of
the control is narrower: it exposes the measurement pipeline's behaviour on
an input with a known answer and puts a bound on how casually a fitted image
slope can be read as material physics.

## Prior-work boundary

Generic two-point microstructure statistics and checkerboard examples already
exist in [PyMKS](https://pymks.readthedocs.io/en/stable/rst/notebooks/checkerboard.html).
[Ledesma-Alonso et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.97.023304)
studied image decimation effects on correlation descriptors. This control is
a reproducibility and interpretation check for **this project's estimator**,
not a claim to have discovered image-resolution bias.
