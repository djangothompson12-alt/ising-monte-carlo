# Pilot: the measured growth rate depends on the observable

Exploratory result, 10 September 2026. Generated by the coding assistant and
awaiting independent student review. This is not a completed long-run result.

The completed 24-replica pilot was reanalysed without changing any simulated
trajectory. For c=0.50, L=96, four independent replicas, the nominal
1,000–20,000-sweep window gives:

| Observable | Effective exponent | 95% whole-replica bootstrap interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original 0.5 correlation crossing | 0.243 | 0.235–0.251 |
| Positive correlation-lobe integral | 0.236 | 0.227–0.245 |
| Full-spectrum inverse first moment | 0.156 | 0.150–0.162 |
| Inverse interface-fraction proxy | 0.199 | 0.190–0.207 |

All four use the same retained checkpoints and the same replica resampling.
These intervals quantify replica variation, not systematic measurement error
or uncertainty in the theoretical model. Four replicas are a small ensemble.

The paired spectral-minus-threshold difference is about −0.087, with a
bootstrap interval of −0.088 to −0.083. This establishes a discrepancy between
these defined measurements on this ensemble. It does not identify its cause,
prove different universal exponents, or overturn the late-stage 1/3 theory.
High-frequency/thermal power is a candidate explanation to investigate, not
something isolated by this comparison.

There is also a useful failure case: at c=0.50, L=32, the strict four-observable
common mask leaves insufficient late-window coverage for a fit. Some positive
correlation lobes do not reach zero within the sampled range. That failure is
retained as NaN rather than assigning a truncated integral or silently dropping
a replica. The primary threshold-only fit and the four-observable comparison
can therefore use different masks; compare their actual retained times.
