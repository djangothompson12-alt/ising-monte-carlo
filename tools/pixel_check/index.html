What the bands are based on

The check measures the two-point correlation length: how far apart two pixels can be and still usually be in the same phase, read where the correlation falls to one half, averaged over the horizontal and vertical directions. It is shorter than a feature's full width, so at one pixel a feature still spans a few pixels; it just looks blocky.

LengthWhat happened to fitted exponents in tests
under 1 pxAlways too slow: by 0.05 to 0.16 across simulations and two real X-ray datasets (Al–Ge, Al–Cu).
1 to 2 pxTypically 0.02 to 0.025 too slow on real images.
2 to 3 pxWithin about 0.01 for sharp images. Half to one pixel of blur pushed errors to 0.02 to 0.04.
3 px and overWithin about 0.01, even with a pixel of blur.

The series check coarsens your own images by a further 2× (block averaging, then thresholding at the midpoint) and refits. If that moves the exponent by more than 0.01, your images sit close to where resolution starts to bias the fit.

Limits

The evidence comes from 2D lattice simulations and slices of two public tomography datasets, not SEM, and no laboratory has validated this check. It flags risk; it does not correct an exponent, and coarsening a single image further was not accurate enough to predict the size of the error. The closest published rule is the resolution criterion of Ledesma-Alonso et al. (Phys. Rev. E 97, 023304, 2018), which in these tests asked for roughly 1.2 to 1.9 pixels.
